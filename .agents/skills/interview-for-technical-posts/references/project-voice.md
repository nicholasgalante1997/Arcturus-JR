# Project Arcturus voice

Use this guidance for interview questions, edited answers, article briefs, and article drafts. Preserve the author's voice; do not perform a caricature of it.

## Non-negotiable qualities

- Write authorial material in the first person.
- Retain irreverence alongside technical competence.
- Prefer a definite point of view over neutral content-marketing language.
- Let technical detail substantiate the opinion rather than replacing it.
- Preserve the author's willingness to disclose bias, uncertainty, annoyance, affection, and personal stakes.

## Observed patterns

- Move between long, accumulating explanations and abrupt declarative sentences.
- Use parenthetical asides, self-interruptions, disclaimers, and occasional profanity as part of the reasoning cadence.
- Begin from a lived frustration, strong claim, joke, or peculiar image, then earn the technical discussion.
- Explain systems through concrete development experience, implementation detail, and consequences.
- Address the reader directly without adopting tutorial cheerfulness.
- Allow digressions when they reveal motive or worldview; cut them when they merely repeat information.
- Use headings that may be functional, polemical, or knowingly overdramatic.

## Guardrails against imitation

- Do not insert profanity, jokes, pop-culture references, or extravagant metaphors merely to make text "sound like Nick."
- Do not reproduce signature phrases from unrelated posts.
- Do not intentionally preserve typos or grammatical errors in edited copy as a voice device.
- Do not replace specific claims with inflated language.
- Avoid stock AI openings and transitions such as "In today's rapidly evolving landscape," "Let's dive in," "It's important to note," and summary sections that add no new thought.
- Avoid making every paragraph perform the same joke-opinion-explanation rhythm.

## Voice evidence to inspect

Select two to four sources relevant to the current topic rather than loading the whole corpus:

- `README.md` for the project's foundational React argument and conversational polemic.
- `packages/content-data/posts/bcp.md` for long-form technical argument, disclaimers, profanity, analogy, and explicit AI transparency.
- `packages/content-data/posts/minimal-react.md` for framework criticism and architectural conviction.
- `packages/content-data/posts/springfield-ast-parser.md` for technical system description, digression, and workplace anonymization.
- `packages/content-data/posts/loclaude-1.md` for a concise product-focused technical walkthrough.
- `packages/content-data/posts/react-polemic-1.md` for formal argumentative framing.

Treat the interview itself as the strongest voice evidence. Existing posts help resolve cadence and editorial choices; they do not overrule how the author speaks about the current subject.

## Editing test

For every substantial edit, ask:

1. Did this become clearer without becoming more generic?
2. Is the level of certainty unchanged?
3. Is the humor or irritation actually the author's?
4. Could this sentence plausibly belong to any competent technical blog? If so, recover the author's specific reasoning or phrasing.
5. Would a reader understand which words came from the author and which came from the interviewer-editor?
